# Functional cables

A cable can carry a step pattern and a pass probability, locked to the patch clock. Rationale and rejected alternatives: `docs/decisions.md`, "Functional cables" (2026-10-09). Cost: `docs/benchmarks.md`.

## Use

Right-click a jack cable and choose "Pattern & probability...", or press "Pattern" on a route row in the routing drawer. Set Steps (0 = no pattern, up to 16), Chance (whole cable) and, per step, a level and a chance. Steps advance on each pulse of the patch's Clock (a 16th note); a Restart starts them again. A patch with no Clock passes everything.

A cable that carries a pattern or a chance below 100 % shows three small bars on the wire.

## Hear it

`echo-throws.mp3` and `five-against-eight.mp3`: eight seconds with plain cables, a second of silence, then the patch as saved. The patches are in `patches/functional-cables/` (listed in the browser as pieces; both play by themselves) and are generated by `cargo run -p kabl-ui --example functional_cable_demos`.

- Echo Throws: the cable from the voice to the delay opens on steps 1, 4 and 7 of 8 and passes 60 % of the time, so echoes are thrown at some notes. The pitch cable passes 70 % of the time; otherwise the last note keeps sounding.
- Five Against Eight: a 5-step pattern on the route into the filter cutoff against an 8-step bass line (they realign every 40 steps), and a 16-step gate on the pad's cable.

The clips were not listened to by the agent that made them (it has no ears); they were checked for level, determinism and difference from the plain version only.

## Morph (prototype, awaiting Kosta)

The repository never says what morph is, so this is a prototype of the three candidates that were put to Kosta, built so they can be heard side by side. Kosta has not chosen; none of this is a decision.

- **A/B blend (candidate 2).** A cable holds a second pattern B (`b.length`, `b.s1..`, `b.r1..`) and `morph` 0..1. Each pulse draws once; the pass chance and the level are the mix of the A step and the B step (each pattern keeps its own length, so a morph between 4 and 5 steps is a polymeter morph). Morph 0 is exactly the old behaviour.
- **Glide (candidate 1).** `glide_ms` slews the level between steps instead of jumping (audio always slews 1 ms at least).
- **Timed transition (candidate 3).** No new machinery: morph is a runtime value, so any ramp over bars (here `render_morph` in the demo example, in the app a macro or CC route once params are mappable) is the transition.

`morph-arc.mp3` (58 s, 24 bars): a slow A-minor piece (bass and pad voiced high enough for small speakers). The delay send, the pad's gate and the bass filter each hold an A pattern (sparse) and a B pattern (busy), and their morphs move on separate schedules (echo bars 4-9 up, 18-24 down; pad 8-16 up, 20-24 down; bass 12-18 up, 21-24 down). The saved patch `patches/functional-cables/morph-arc` sits at morph 0.3; in the app, open a cable's Pattern editor and move "Morph A to B".

Not decided: whether blending chances this way sounds like a real in-between to a person, and whether morph should be a cable property at all or a separate source that several cables follow.
