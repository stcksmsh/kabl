# Contributing to kabl

Draft contributor terms, for the owner's decision. Not legal advice.

kabl is GPL-3.0-or-later. The owner named in `COPYRIGHT` holds the copyright of the whole
project and must be able to relicense, sell or transfer it. Contributions are therefore accepted
only under the terms below.

## Terms (licence grant)

By sending a contribution (a pull request, patch or any other submission) you agree that:

1. You wrote it, or you have the right to submit it, and you are not sending anyone else's code
   or other material without saying so in the pull request.
2. You keep the copyright of your contribution.
3. You grant the owner a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, irrevocable licence to
   use, copy, modify, publish, distribute and **sublicense** your contribution, and to relicense
   it under any terms, including terms that are not open source, and to transfer this licence to
   a successor or buyer of the project.
4. The contribution is also available to everyone under the project licence, GPL-3.0-or-later.
5. You grant a patent licence for your contribution on the same terms as in point 3.
6. You waive, or agree not to assert against the owner and its licensees, moral rights to the
   extent the law allows.
7. If you used an AI tool, you say so in the pull request.

Every commit carries a sign-off line, `git commit -s`:
`Signed-off-by: Your Name <you@example.org>`. The sign-off states that you agree to these terms.
The first pull request of a contributor also says "I agree to the terms in CONTRIBUTING.md".

No bot or service checks this. The maintainer checks it by hand and does not merge a pull
request without it.

## Why this and not the alternatives

| Option | Keeps sale or relicensing open | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| DCO only (sign-off) | no: contributors stay licensors under the project licence only, so relicensing needs each of them | none |
| CLA with licence grant (above) | yes | one paragraph and a sign-off |
| Copyright assignment | yes, strongest | needs a signed document per person, and not every country lets authors assign (moral rights); contributors dislike it |

Recommendation: the licence grant, with the DCO sign-off as the record. A lawyer should confirm
the wording and its validity where the owner and the contributors live.
